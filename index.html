Bioinformatics 101 for colon model experiments

A three-day, hands-on introduction to the computational skills needed to analyse data from colon model experiments. The school is designed for complete beginners: no prior bioinformatics experience is required.

What the course covers

  • Introduction to the command line and bash/Unix
  • Introduction to R
  • Statistical analysis for colon model experiments
  • Overview of the data produced in colon models: microbiome and metabolome
  • Introduction to code management with GitHub
  • Workflows, environments, and FAIR data management

Who it is for

The school is aimed at Early Career Researchers (PhD students and postdocs), but is open to all, and technical personnel are particularly welcome. It focuses on people who are already working with in-vitro colon models, or who are planning to.

  • No prior experience is needed. The course starts from the very beginning.
  • If the command line and coding make you nervous, this school is designed for you. Participants who feel apprehensive about code are especially encouraged to apply.
  • 30 places available.

What to bring

  • Your own laptop. No machines are provided.
  • The ability to install software on it. You will need RStudio and a number of bioinformatics packages, so you must have administrator rights, or have your IT department install them beforehand. Installation instructions will be sent to participants ahead of the school.
  • At least 8 GB of RAM. 16 GB is recommended.

Eligibility and how to register

Eligibility criteria

  • Open to everyone, but Early Career Researchers will be prioritised.
  • Participants who registered for the Second INFOGUT Training School in Clermont-Ferrand (2026) are not eligible for this school.
  • Travel and daily allowance costs are covered by the INFOGUT COST Action. Only participants from COST countries are eligible for this reimbursement.
